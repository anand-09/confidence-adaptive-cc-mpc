Confidence-Adaptive Chance-Constrained Model Predictive Control for Autonomous Driving

Anand Singh1, Johannes Betz2, Mattia Piccinini2

Estimate-then-plan CC-MPC · per-agent MAP confidence · online risk δ(c)

Paper Code Watch demos

Abstract

Autonomous vehicles must predict surrounding traffic participants to plan safely, but prediction degrades during sudden braking, cut-ins, or occlusion events such as a pedestrian hidden behind a parked vehicle. Existing chance-constrained model predictive control planners fix their safety risk level in advance, so the margin kept around other agents does not adapt to the trustworthiness of current predictions. We propose Confidence-Adaptive Stochastic Predictive Control, which introduces a per-agent confidence state derived from a regularized estimation objective to adjust the risk level online. We show that this yields a distributionally robust safety guarantee under a stated residual-calibration assumption, establish recursive feasibility and stability via robust control theory, and derive a performance guarantee relative to fixed-risk baselines. We identify and correct a bias arising when confidence is optimized jointly with control, and extend the framework to multi-agent scenes through an optimal risk-allocation rule. Closed-loop simulations on three urban driving scenarios, a braking cut-in, an occluded pedestrian, and a multi-agent intersection, together with a 100-trial randomized Monte Carlo study, demonstrate reduced conservatism compared with fixed-risk baselines while matching or improving their empirical violation rates. The implementation and experimental code are publicly available at https://github.com/anand-09/confidence-adaptive-cc-mpc.

Key Contributions

1

MAP confidence law

A derived MAP update for per-agent confidence whose Cantelli back-off solves a DRO chance constraint, with recursive feasibility, practical stability, and a performance sandwich versus fixed-δ CC-MPC.

2

Joint-optimization bias

Optimizing confidence inside the QP is optimistically biased when the constraint is active (Theorem 3). Estimate-then-plan with a trust-region patch restores the safety guarantee.

3

Optimal multi-agent split

A confidence-weighted allocation uniquely minimizes a generalized KL objective under a conserved scene risk budget, shifting conservatism toward the less reliable agent.

4

Closed-loop evidence

Three public-protocol urban scenes, literature tightening baselines, 100-trial Monte Carlo, and a Theorem 3 grid. Adaptive cuts mean back-off 41–48% at zero violations.

Methods

1

From prediction error to confidence

The one-step residual ek is normalized to yk ∈ [0,1]. Confidence ck is the minimizer of a three-term regularized objective (prior, data-fit, decay), which is MAP estimation and yields the closed form ck+1 = (1−β)ck + α yk.

Confidence code
2

From confidence to risk

Fixed δ is replaced by δ(c) = δmin + (δmax−δmin)(1−e−λc). Effective residual scale σeff(c) and Cantelli back-off ρ(c) then tighten the chance constraint only when the forecast is untrustworthy.

Risk code
3

Estimate-then-plan (no joint bias)

Deployed control sets ck = ĉk and never solves the naive joint program (Pnaive). Theorem 3 shows that jointly optimizing c with u overstates confidence when the constraint is active; a trust-region restores safety.

CC-MPC code
4

Multi-agent risk allocation

With a conserved δtotal, the split δ̃k(i) ∝ δ(ck(i)) uniquely minimizes a generalized KL objective and, by Boole, keeps the union violation probability ≤ δtotal.
