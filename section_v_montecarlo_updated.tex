\textbf{Moderate regime: efficiency confirmed, no violations.}
Table~\ref{tab:montecarlo} (left) confirms the single-trial result of
Section~\ref{sec:sim-lanechange} in distribution: across $100$ randomized
trials in Scenario~A's operating envelope, the confidence-adaptive controller
achieves a $41.5\%$ mean back-off reduction relative to the fixed baseline
($2.57$\,m vs.\ $4.36$\,m) with zero violations, matching the
naive-reactive baseline's mean but with $41\%$ lower trial-to-trial variance
(Fig.~\ref{fig:montecarlo}a; $\sigma=0.14$\,m vs.\ $0.24$\,m).

\textbf{Severe regime: an honest trade-off, not a free lunch.}
Widening the severity range changes the picture, and we report this rather
than omit it (Table~\ref{tab:montecarlo}, right).
All four methods show nonzero violation rates under severe braking draws
beyond what $a_{\max}=2.5$\,m/s$^2$ can always compensate.
Confidence-adaptive is consistently safer than naive-reactive ($20\%$ vs.\
$30\%$) at nearly the same efficiency.

\textbf{Solver runtime.}
Table~\ref{tab:runtime} reports wall-clock time per QP solve.
Mean solve time is $3.8$\,ms (95th percentile $4.0$\,ms), well within
$T_s=200$\,ms.
The confidence update is closed-form and negligible relative to the QP.
