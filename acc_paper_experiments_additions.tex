\textbf{Moderate regime: efficiency confirmed, no violations.}
Table~\ref{tab:montecarlo} (left) confirms the single-trial result of
Section~\ref{sec:sim-lanechange} in distribution: across $100$ randomized
trials of Scenario~A (braking severity, onset, duration, and measurement noise
randomized per trial), comparing fixed-$\delta=0.05$, worst-case-fixed
($\delta=\delta_{\min}$), naive-reactive (raw $\bar e_k\!\to\!\delta$ without
Proposition~1), and confidence-adaptive, the adaptive controller achieves a
$41.5\%$ mean back-off reduction vs.\ fixed ($2.57$\,m vs.\ $4.36$\,m) with
zero violations, matching naive-reactive's mean but with $41\%$ lower
trial-to-trial variance (Fig.~\ref{fig:montecarlo}a; $\sigma=0.14$\,m vs.\
$0.24$\,m), consistent with the regularized MAP estimator smoothing a purely
reactive rule.

\textbf{Severe regime: an honest trade-off, not a free lunch.}
Widening severity (deceleration $\in[2.0,5.0]$\,m/s$^2$, onset
$\in[3.0,5.0]$\,s, duration $\in[1.0,3.0]$\,s) yields nonzero violations
for all methods (Table~\ref{tab:montecarlo}, right), including worst-case
fixed ($10\%$), indicating some trials exceed what $a_{\max}=2.5$\,m/s$^2$
can compensate; this reflects the scenario distribution, not a particular
risk policy. Margin-reducing methods show higher violation rates (naive
$30\%$, adaptive $25\%$) than conservative ones (fixed $20\%$, worst-case
$10\%$), the expected cost of relaxing margins during calm periods
(Theorem~\ref{thm:feasible}). Adaptive is consistently safer than naive
(lower violation rate and variance), but adaptation is not a substitute for
choosing $\delta_{\min}$, $\delta_{\max}$, $\kappa$ conservatively enough---a
calibration question flagged as future work.

\textbf{Solver runtime.}
Table~\ref{tab:runtime} reports wall-clock QP time pooled over ${\approx}40{,}000$
solves (both regimes, four methods). Mean $3.8$\,ms, median $3.8$\,ms, 95th
$4.0$\,ms---well within $T_s=200$\,ms. Max $111$\,ms occurs on OSQP-fallback
(SCS) instances; a deployed system would use a solve-time watchdog (reuse
previous input or emergency maneuver).

\subsection{Parameter Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

Parameters $\alpha$, $\beta$ (Proposition~1), $\lambda$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-of-c}),
and $\kappa$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma-eff}) each play a distinct role.
Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity} sweeps one parameter at a time on Scenario~A
(adaptive controller), holding others at nominal values
($\alpha=0.30$, $\beta=0.33$, $\lambda=3$, $\kappa=0.6$).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_sensitivity.pdf}
\caption{Parameter sensitivity, Scenario~A (adaptive). Each panel varies one
parameter; dashed lines mark nominal values. Mean back-off varies smoothly;
nominal settings lie in flat regions, indicating robustness to moderate
retuning. Fixed-$\delta=0.05$ baseline: $4.36$\,m mean back-off.}
\label{fig:sensitivity}
\end{figure}

Larger $\alpha$ reacts faster to errors (faster erosion and recovery);
larger $\beta$ damps swings. $\lambda$ and $\kappa$ affect back-off
monotonically, consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:feasible}. No tested value
violated safety on the representative trajectory; severe-regime Monte Carlo
(Table~\ref{tab:montecarlo}, right) remains the distribution-level stress test.
